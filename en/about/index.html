Tirana, Albania

One city. A thousand stories.

A short guide to the city: how it began, how it looks, and how it lives today.

History

Tirana was founded in 1614 by Sulejman Pasha Bargjini, who built a mosque, a bakery and a hammam where caravan routes met. For three centuries it stayed a small market town, until the Congress of Lushnjë chose it as Albania's capital in 1920.

In the 1930s the centre was redesigned with wide boulevards and government buildings. From 1944 to 1991 the communist regime shaped the city again: bunkers, mosaics and the closed party quarter of Blloku all date from those years.

Skanderbeg Square
02

Architecture

Walk along Dëshmorët e Kombit boulevard and you pass Ottoman, Italian-era, socialist and glass-and-steel buildings in twenty minutes. The ministries around Skanderbeg Square date from the 1930s, the Pyramid from 1988.

In the early 2000s the city painted many grey apartment blocks in bold colours and patterns. The project changed how Tirana looks and gave it one of its best-known images.

Pyramid of Tirana
03

Culture

Museums such as Bunk'Art and the House of Leaves turn a difficult past into places to learn and remember. The National Gallery, independent galleries and street art keep the present in view.

Religious life is calm and mixed: the Et'hem Bey Mosque, the Orthodox Resurrection Cathedral and the Catholic St Paul's Cathedral all stand within walking distance of each other.

House of Leaves
04

Food

Albanian cooking is built on what grows nearby: tavë kosi (baked lamb with yoghurt), fërgesë, byrek, grilled meat and fresh vegetables from markets like Pazari i Ri.

Coffee is a ritual. People meet for an espresso at any hour, and a short coffee can easily turn into a long conversation.

New Bazaar
05

Urban life

Tirana is young and lives outdoors. Early evening brings the xhiro, a slow walk along the boulevards, before cafés and bars fill Blloku late into the night.

When the city needs to breathe, it heads to the Grand Park and its lake, or takes the cable car up Mount Dajti for the view.
